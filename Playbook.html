Stage Dynamics & Moving Average Behavior

Mapping the transition from stable trend regimes into compression and distribution zones.

Stable Configurations vs. Transition Compression Zones

Stage 2 and Stage 4 are stable configurations — the moving averages hold a consistent stacked order (rising 50 > 150 > 200 in Stage 2; falling 50 < 150 < 200 in Stage 4) for the entire duration of the stage. That stability is what makes them easy to define crisply with price and MA rules.

Stage 3 is a transition/compression zone, not a stable one. It only exists because the clean Stage 2 stack is breaking down — so by definition it has no single fixed MA arrangement. It is diagnosed by what is unraveling, not by a new stable pattern forming.

Moving Average / Metric Stage 2 Behavior (Uptrend) Stage 3 Behavior (Transition / Topping)
50-day MA Price holds consistently above it; line is steadily rising Price crosses above/below it repeatedly (whipsaw); slope flattens — fastest to react, so it signals first
150-day MA Rising cleanly, price tracking above it Starts flattening, but lags the 50 MA's flattening by weeks or months
200-day MA Rising cleanly, price tracking above it Usually still rising through most of Stage 3 — slowest to react, often the last one to show change
MA Order (50 > 150 > 200) Clearly fanned out with wide separation Order may still technically hold, but MAs converge and compress toward each other instead of fanning out
Price vs. Prior Highs Consistent higher-highs and higher-lows First lower high appears, even while price may still sit above the 200 MA

Stage 2 → Stage 3 Transition Conditions

Stage 3 (distribution/topping) begins when an orderly Stage 2 uptrend loses its clean character. Monitor these specific topping signatures:

Choppy Price Action: Wide, overlapping swings replace smooth higher-highs and higher-lows.
Churning Volume: Volume dries up on up-days and spikes on down-days; heavy volume stalls with no net price progress.
50-day MA Roll: The 50-day moving average flattens, then starts rolling over after months of sustained ascent.
First Lower High: Formation of the first lower high breaks the unbroken sequence of Stage 2 higher highs.
RS Line Deterioration: The Relative Strength line rolls over, showing clear underperformance versus the index while price hovers near highs.
Climactic Run: Parabolic vertical advance accompanied by massive exhaustion volume often flags the terminal blow-off.
Break of Structure (BoS): Loss of the last major higher-low confirms Stage 3 distribution is locked in, not merely a pullback.
The "Question Mark" Stage: Stage 3 doesn't have its own stable "shape" the way Stage 2 and Stage 4 do — it is defined by the loss of Stage 2's clean conditions (50 MA flattening, whipsaws, convergence) while Stage 4's conditions haven't triggered yet. That is why Stan Weinstein calls it the "question mark" stage: you can only say "this looks like topping" during it, not "this is confirmed Stage 3" with the same mathematical certainty as Stage 2.
Unambiguous Stage 4 Confirmation Trigger: The one decisive trigger that ends Stage 3 and confirms Stage 4 (decline) is the 50-day MA crossing down through the 150-day MA, with price decisively losing the 200-day MA. Until that crossover happens, everything in between is inherently a transitional gray zone.
1. Risk-Management Stops

Always-Active Capital Defense

  • Initial stop: Placed 5–10% below entry or just below pivot point; sized to absolute portfolio risk rather than open P&L.
  • Breakeven adjustment: Trail stop up to breakeven once price moves favorably into profit.
  • 50-Day hard exit: Violation of the 50-day MA on heavy institutional distribution volume triggers an automatic exit.
2. Price-Action Exits

Selling Into Strength

  • Climax top: Parabolic move on exhaustion volume — sell into strength; do not wait for reversal confirmation.
  • Churning action: Abnormally heavy volume with zero price progress over multiple sessions requires scaling out.
  • Trendline failure: Breakdown through the primary ascending trendline connecting swing lows.
  • Failed breakout: Pokes to marginal new highs on weak/declining volume and quickly reverses.
3. Structural Exits

Market Structure & Velocity

  • BoS breakdown: Violation of the last significant higher-low (BoS to the downside) invalidates trend structure.
  • Extended Stage 3 chop: Protracted, non-accumulative sideways action triggers a capital redeployment exit to eliminate opportunity cost.
4. Relative Strength Exits

Loss of Market Leadership

  • RS line divergence: RS line registers new cyclical lows while price remains resilient — an indicator of silent institutional distribution prior to price breakdown.
5. Fundamental Exits

Deteriorating Fundamentals

  • Earnings deceleration: Meaningful growth slowdown or earnings miss following prior multi-quarter acceleration.
  • Sponsorship loss: Net institutional liquidation visible via consecutive block deal disclosures and fund reductions.

Execution Workflow

1

Market Gate (M)

Confirm the general market is in a confirmed uptrend before committing capital.

2

Technical Filter

Screen for Stage 2 Trend Template compliance and tight volatility contraction bases (VCP).

3

Fundamental Test

Verify quarterly earnings acceleration (C & A) and top-tier group strength (L).

4

Manage & Exit

Enforce strict initial stops, monitor Stage 3 topping tells, and exit on rule violations.

Risk Management Rule: Never average down on a losing position. If moving average health lines or structural higher-lows break on volume, respect the exit rules regardless of fundamental thesis.

What Can Be Screened?

Mostly Measurable

  • Moving-average and 52-week price conditions
  • Current-quarter and annual EPS growth trends
  • Relative strength rating and volume dry-up signatures

Needs Manual Confirmation

  • New catalyst quality, products, or capex execution
  • Subtle institutional churn versus clean accumulation
  • Broader market distribution day counts and stage posture
