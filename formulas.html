Stock Analysis Formulas: One Page

Quality + Growth + Reasonable Valuation. Rs. crore for company totals, Rs. for per-share values. Good levels are rules of thumb.

Profit ladder: Sales - Expenses = Operating profit → - Depreciation + Other income = EBIT → - Interest = Profit before tax → - Tax = Net profit → ÷ Shares = EPS

1. Profit and margins

Operating profitSales - Expenses (before depreciation, interest)Core business profit
OPM %Operating profit / Sales x 100Stable or rising
EBITDANet profit + Interest + Tax + Depreciation= Operating profit + Other income
EBITNet profit + Interest + Tax
or EBITDA - Depreciation		Core only: Operating profit - Depreciation
Profit before taxEBIT - Interest
Net profit marginNet profit / Sales x 100Higher than peers
Tax rateTax / Profit before tax x 100Watch very low rates
Other income shareOther income / Profit before tax x 100Below 10-15%

2. Per-share values

Number of sharesEquity capital / Face valueUse weighted average
EPS(Net profit - Preference dividend) / SharesPrefer diluted EPS
Book value / share(Equity capital + Reserves) / Shares
Market capPrice x Shares
Dividend payout %Dividend / Net profit x 100
Retention ratio1 - Payout ratio

3. Returns on capital (quality)

Net worthEquity capital + ReservesShareholders' funds
ROE %Net profit / Net worth x 10015%+ for years. Use average net worth
Capital employedNet worth + Borrowings
or Total assets - Current liabilities
ROCE %EBIT / Capital employed x 10015-20%+, steady
ROA %Net profit / Total assets x 100Banks: 1.5%+ is strong
DuPont ROENet margin x Asset turnover x LeverageShows where ROE comes from
Asset turnoverSales / Total assets
LeverageTotal assets / Net worthHigh = debt-driven ROE

4. Safety and cash flow

Debt to equityBorrowings / Net worthBelow 1 (non-financial)
Interest coverEBIT / InterestAbove 3-4
Net debtBorrowings - Cash and investments
Net debt / EBITDANet debt / EBITDABelow 2-3
Cash conversion (CFO/PAT)Operating cash flow / Net profit70-100%+ over 3-5 years
Free cash flowOperating cash flow - CapexPositive, growing
FCF yield %FCF / Market cap x 100

5. Working capital

Receivable daysReceivables / Sales x 365Should not rise faster than sales
Inventory daysInventory / Cost of goods x 365Stable
Payable daysPayables / Cost of goods x 365
Cash conversion cycleReceivable + Inventory - Payable daysShorter is better

6. Growth

YoY growth %(This period / Same period last year - 1) x 100Use YoY for quarters
CAGR %[(End / Start)^(1/Years) - 1] x 100Do for 3, 5, 10 years
Sustainable growthROE x Retention ratioGrowth far above needs debt or new shares
Operating leverageProfit growth % / Sales growth %Above 1 if margins improve

7. Valuation

P/EPrice / EPS
or Market cap / Net profit		Compare with own history, peers
Earnings yield %EPS / Price x 100 (= 1 / P/E)Compare with FD or bond yield
PEGP/E / EPS growth % (annual)1-1.5 fair, above 2 expensive
P/BPrice / Book value per shareBanks, asset-heavy firms
Enterprise value (EV)Market cap + Borrowings - Cash
EV/EBITDAEV / EBITDADebt-heavy, capital-intensive
Price/SalesMarket cap / SalesLoss-making firms
Dividend yield %Dividend per share / Price x 100
Premium to own history(Current P/E / Median P/E - 1) x 100Above 20-25% needs reason

8. Expected return and price plan

Future EPSEPS x (1 + g)^ng = growth rate, n = years
Future priceFuture EPS x Exit P/EUse a conservative exit P/E
Expected return %[(Future price / Price)^(1/n) - 1] x 100 + Dividend yieldCompare with required 12-15%
Margin of safety %(Estimated value - Price) / Estimated value x 100Higher is safer
Risk-reward ratio(Target - Entry) / (Entry - Stop level)2 or more
Rally check %(Price now / Price 3 months ago - 1) x 100Above 40%: wait
Tranche sizeTotal planned amount / Number of tranches (3-4)Write before buying

Example, GE Shipping FY26: Net profit 2,943 / Net worth 16,963 gives ROE 17.3% (on year-end net worth). EBIT 3,162 / Capital employed 18,050 gives ROCE 17.5%. Screener's own ratios may use different bases, so keep one method for all stocks. Banks and NBFCs use ROA, ROE, P/B and NPAs instead of ROCE and D/E. For study only, not investment advice.
